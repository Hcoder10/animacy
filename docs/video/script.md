# animacy — demo film script

Two hosts, podcast format. **LAMP** = Autonomous Lamp (dry, deadpan, slightly proud).
**REACHY** = Reachy Mini (quicker, warmer, finishes his sentences).

Rules for the edit: no title cards that animate, no whoosh transitions, no
stock music stings. Hard cuts, a few J-cuts (audio leads the picture), one or
two dissolves at section boundaries only. Lower-thirds are small, static, and
appear once per idea. Everything on screen is real: real terminal output, the
real viewer, the real robot, the real dataset page.

Every line below is spoken by a robot whose motion is generated by animacy from
that same audio. Nothing is keyframed.

---

## 1 — Cold open (over: both hosts idle on the set, one small look between them)

**LAMP:** Every expressive robot ships with a menu of hand-made moves. I have thirty-one.

**REACHY:** I have eighty-five.

**LAMP:** And when the menu runs out, we repeat ourselves.

**REACHY:** Animacy is the other way round. Motion comes from people, and any robot can wear it.

**LAMP:** Including a lamp.

---

## 2 — Capture (over: a licensed talking-head video with the tracker overlay, then the channel bars moving)

**REACHY:** Step one. A person talking on video, or on your webcam.

**LAMP:** Face and body become twenty-eight channels at thirty hertz. Head in six degrees of freedom, gaze, brows, mouth, torso, an arm — and a flag for who is speaking.

**REACHY:** That is the canonical space. It is the only thing the rest of the system speaks.

**LAMP:** No robot appears anywhere in it. That is the point.

---

## 3 — One file per robot (over: ROBOT.md scrolling, then `animacy check` passing for three robots)

**REACHY:** Step two. Your robot is one markdown file.

**LAMP:** Joints, limits, rest pose, the vendor's own safety ceiling, and a mapping from those human channels onto your joints.

**REACHY:** My head is a six degree-of-freedom Stewart platform. His is a lamp arm on a bearing. Same file format, same clip, different bodies.

**LAMP:** A coding agent added a third robot — an SO-101 arm — in twenty-six minutes, by following our own instructions. It logged every place the instructions were unclear, and we fixed those.

---

## 4 — Retarget (over: the lamp leaning in while its gaze stays put; the speed-cap plot)

**LAMP:** Step three. Retarget. The gains are fitted from each vendor's own clips, so a human nod becomes a nod the way this body already nods.

**REACHY:** Forward kinematics keeps his gaze on you while he leans in. Springs give the overshoot that makes motion look alive.

**LAMP:** And the speed ceiling is absolute. When a human move is too fast for a servo, time is stretched — never clipped. Zero violations, every clip.

---

## 5 — The interaction layer (over: the two of us, mid-sentence; then the waveform under the motion)

**REACHY:** Step four is what we are doing right now.

**LAMP:** Our speech goes in as audio. Motion comes out on the same clock.

**REACHY:** So it is in sync by construction. Nobody keyframed this conversation. Watch me while he talks — listening is its own behaviour.

**LAMP:** The shipped source is retrieval: real human motion, matched to the speech, chosen by intent.

**REACHY:** There is a learned model too, and the honest answer is that on speakers it has never heard it beats the floors on likelihood and on motion statistics —

**LAMP:** — but not on beat alignment. So retrieval ships, the model stays selectable, and the numbers are in the README.

---

## 6 — On real hardware (over: the physical Reachy Mini moving; the daemon read-back numbers)

**REACHY:** This runs on me. Thirty hertz over the daemon, and the head axes read back within a couple of degrees of what was commanded.

**LAMP:** And for me it writes the exact CSV that Autonomous OS already accepts — same columns, same limits, checked against the vendor's own validator.

**REACHY:** He does not have a body yet.

**LAMP:** I am aware.

---

## 7 — Data (over: the Hugging Face dataset page, the harvest counter ticking)

**REACHY:** All of it comes from licensed video. Public domain and Creative Commons, with the licence evidence stored next to every clip.

**LAMP:** Hundreds of minutes, thirty-seven speakers, twelve languages — and a harvester that runs all night toward five thousand hours.

**REACHY:** Anyone can add to it from a browser tab.

---

## 8 — The judge (over: a grading reel, the score table)

**LAMP:** And we do not grade ourselves. A separate model watches short clips with no idea what made them, next to the vendors' own hand-made animations, and scores them.

**REACHY:** It is blind, the test lines are sealed, and we publish what it says.

**LAMP:** Right now it puts us level with the hand-made clips on some movements and below on others. That is in the README too.

---

## 9 — Close (over: both hosts, a beat of stillness, then the URL)

**REACHY:** One motion space. One file per robot.

**LAMP:** Bring your robot. It is one file.
