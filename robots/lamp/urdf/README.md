# Autonomous Lamp URDF — how it was built and what was verified

`lamp.urdf` is generated by `scripts/lamp_build_urdf.py`; the meshes by
`scripts/lamp_extract_meshes.py`; the preview PNGs by
`scripts/lamp_render_clip.py`. Everything below is the evidence behind the
numbers in those scripts. Nothing has been verified on hardware yet.

## Source geometry

The vendor ships two things in `robots/lamp/cad_src/` (Autonomous OS,
Apache-2.0): per-part STLs and `lamp.glb`, a 22 MB Blender export of the full
assembly. The STLs are clean watertight printed-part shells (millimetres) but
are **not** usable for placement on their own: the base and arm parts are
exported in local, centred frames while the head parts are in an assembly
frame, so nothing tells you where a part sits. The GLB's solids are the
opposite — correctly placed but fragmented open surfaces (the base drum alone
has 14 k boundary edges) that render as shards. So the GLB provides placement
and the STLs provide surfaces (see *Meshes* below). The GLB is the useful file
for kinematics:

- it is one skinned mesh per moving part, in assembled world coordinates
  (glTF y-up, metres, lamp facing +x), named `0_base`, `1_base_yaw`,
  `2_base_pitch`, `3_elbow_pitch`, `4_wrist_roll`, `5_wrist_pitch`, `6_wire`;
- it carries an armature whose bones sit on the joint pivots, with inverse-bind
  matrices equal to the node transforms (so the mesh pose *is* the bind pose);
- it carries an `ArmatureAction` animation (86 frames at 20 Hz, the vendor's
  recording rate): the lamp starts folded back, settles into the bind pose at
  ~1 s, then looks around.

Skin weights map each mesh group to exactly one bone, which is how the link
membership was decided (the group names and bone names are crossed at the
wrist — see below).

## Frames and pivots

URDF world: x forward (the way the head faces at rest), y left, z up, origin
at the centre of the base bottom. GLB → URDF is `(x, y, z) → (x, -z, y)`.
Joint pivots (metres, bind pose), read from the armature and snapped to the
x-z plane (the CAD arm plane is exactly y = 0):

| joint | pivot | axis (bind frame) | from |
|---|---|---|---|
| `base_yaw` | (0, 0, 0.0495) | −z | bone `base_yaw`; the 65 mm turntable disc in the swivel group sits at z = 0.0495–0.0545 |
| `base_pitch` | (0, 0, 0.0966) | +y | bone `base_pitch`; a 19.2 × 37 mm axle solid along y is centred here, and the swivel holds an STS3215 (24.7 × 45.2 × 36.3 mm) whose shaft direction is this axis |
| `elbow_pitch` | (−0.0948, 0, 0.1952) | −y | bone `elbow_pitch`; the same 19.2 × 37 mm axle solid is centred here |
| `wrist_pitch` | (−0.0242, 0, 0.2636) | +y | bone named `wrist_pitch`; the same axle solid again → a pitch axle at the neck base |
| `wrist_roll` | (0.0135, 0, 0.3051) | −(neck axis) = −(0.672, 0, 0.740) | bone named `wrist_roll`; the head group contains an STS3215 whose shaft lies along the neck axis and lands exactly on this point |

Link frames are all *world-aligned at the bind pose* with the origin at the
link's pivot, so every mesh has an identity `<origin>` and each joint origin
is `translate(parent pivot → child pivot) · Rot(axis, −bind_value)`.

### Wrist topology

LeLamp (the ancestor, `onshape-to-robot` URDF read for comparison, not
copied) has roll at the end of the upper arm and pitch in the head. This CAD
is the other way round: **pitch at the neck base, roll into the head**.
Evidence: (1) the neck-base pivot carries the same 19 × 37 mm axle part as the
two arm pitch joints, oriented along y; (2) the only servo body in the head
has its shaft along the neck axis and its shaft end on the head pivot; (3) the
GLB animation pitches the neck-base bone about y and rolls the head bone about
the neck axis; (4) the neck group is skinned to the neck-base bone and the head
group to the head bone. So `wrist_roll` (vendor ID 4) is physically the last
joint and `wrist_pitch` (ID 5) the fourth; the URDF chain is
`base → base_yaw → swivel → base_pitch → lower_arm → elbow_pitch → upper_arm → wrist_pitch → neck → wrist_roll → head`.
The vendor's mesh-group names (`4_wrist_roll` = neck, `5_wrist_pitch` = head)
are named after the servo *inside* each part, not the joint that moves it.

## Joint value convention

The URDF joint values are the vendor's own numbers (the `*.pos` columns of
`hal/recordings/*.csv`, what `/servo/upload` and `/servo/move` take) with
`urdf_sign = 1`, `urdf_offset = 0`, treated as degrees → radians.

### Signs (direction of a positive value)

All five come from the vendor's device-measured notes in
`hal/drivers/tracking/constants.py` (lamp-ac82, 2026-08-25) and
`hal/drivers/tracking/servo_follow.py`, and are reproduced by the URDF
(`tests/test_lamp_urdf.py::test_sign_conventions`):

- `base_yaw` +: pans the camera to the lamp's **right** ("base_yaw −24 → face
  at the far right of frame; +24 → centre-left … increasing either joint pans
  the camera right"). URDF axis −z.
- `wrist_roll` +: also pans **right** (same note). URDF axis = −(neck axis).
  Because the head looks perpendicular to the neck, rolling about the neck
  swings the gaze left/right — the vendor's `headshake`.
- `elbow_pitch` +: camera **up** ("elbow +1.6 framed the desk and +54.8 framed
  the ceiling"). URDF axis −y, i.e. the fold closes and the head rises.
- `wrist_pitch` +: head tips **down** ("Looking up drives wrist NEGATIVE").
  URDF axis +y.
- `base_pitch` +: lower arm leans **forward**, camera down (base_pitch shares
  the wrist's sign in the pitch distribution; only the elbow is flipped,
  `ELBOW_PITCH_SIGN = -1`, so base and elbow have opposite senses). URDF axis
  +y. The `AIM_DESK` preset (base_pitch 41) vs `AIM_WALL` (19) agrees.
- `AIM_LEFT` = yaw −91.6 and `AIM_RIGHT` = +88.4 in `hal/presets.py` agree with
  the yaw sign.

### Zero / offset

What is *not* documented anywhere by the vendor is which vendor values the CAD
pose corresponds to (calibration sets zero at a hand-chosen "middle of range"
pose). Two candidates were scored by running FK over all 31 vendor clips:

- **CAD pose = vendor home pose (chosen)**: base_pitch 29.8, elbow 27.1,
  wrist_pitch −26.3, wrist_roll 8.2, yaw 0 (the value every clip starts from and
  returns to; yaw's −2.4 is treated as 0 because the CAD arm plane is exactly
  y = 0). With this, `stretching` is the tallest clip (head pivot 0.37 m),
  `sad`/`sleepy` are the lowest (0.22 m), `wake_up` starts lying back (0.22 m)
  and rises, idle is an upright lamp at 0.32 m looking forward and slightly
  down. It also matches two vendor artefacts: the original "from design"
  `idle.csv` (git 8fc305a) has base_pitch ≈ 0 and wrist_roll ≈ 0 exactly where
  the shipped clips have 29.8 and 8.2, and the GLB's own animation starts
  from a lying-back pose 47° behind the bind pose, like `wake_up`
  (base_pitch 3 → 42).
- CAD pose = vendor zero (rejected): idle would look 17° *up* at 0.39 m and
  `stretching` would barely rise (+4 cm); the tracking demo pose (all zeros)
  would however look more natural under it. If hardware shows the lamp
  standing more upright than the previews, this is the knob: change `BIND_Q`
  in `scripts/lamp_build_urdf.py` and regenerate.

### Units caveat (important)

The vendor's values are **not true degrees**. `hal/follower/config_hal_follower.py`
has `use_degrees = False`, so the bus runs LeRobot's `RANGE_M100_100` mode:
−100…100 spans each joint's calibrated `range_min…range_max`
(`hal/presets.py`: "These numbers are NOT angles"). With the shipped
`hal/calibration/robots/hal_follower/hal.json` that is ≈1.07°/unit for
`base_yaw`, ≈1.16°/unit for `wrist_roll`, and for the pitch joints depends on
the per-unit sweep (1.8°/unit if the full 0…4095 default is kept, which the
clips clearly were not recorded under — base_pitch 74 would be 133°). The
vendor changed calibration on 2026-08-21 and re-issued the recordings on
2026-08-24, and its own notes say the two disagree on a real unit. The URDF
therefore treats 1 unit = 1° (exact for nothing, within ~15 % for yaw/roll,
unknown for the pitch joints). Amplitudes in the previews are approximate;
directions and topology are not affected.

  `C:\Users\sarta\reachy-duplex\.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m pytest tests/test_lamp_urdf.py`.

- `preview/*.png` (rendered by `scripts/lamp_render_clip.py`, which goes
  through `animacy.export.read_autonomous_os_csv` → `animacy.retarget.to_urdf_values`):
  `rest_idle_first` upright lamp, head pivot 0.32 m, looking forward/slightly
  down; `sad_extreme` head 0.22 m looking straight down at the desk;
  `sleepy_extreme` head 0.23 m drooped over the base; `nod_extreme` a bob to
  0.33 m; `headshake_min`/`_max` head turned left/right; `stretching_extreme`
  head 0.36 m, arm nearly vertical, the tallest pose of the clip set;
  `curious_extreme` head yawed and rolled; `wake_up_first` lying back, head
  0.22 m; `zero_pose` (all vendor zeros) lying back looking down;
  `contact_sheet.png` has all of them.
- Not verified: anything on hardware (signs are the vendor's measurements,
  not ours), the exact unit scale, and the exact zero offset (see above).

## Meshes

`../meshes/*.stl`, metres, one per link (see `../meshes/ATTRIBUTION.md`).
Surfaces are the vendor's per-part STLs; each part-assembly that shares a
local frame (`base`+`base-cap`+`button`; the three `swivel-part`s; the two
halves of each arm; `neck`; `cap-servo`; the three `head-part`s + `light-cover`)
is placed by rigid registration onto the matching solid of `lamp.glb`
(PCA-initialised point-to-point ICP, four proper sign combinations, best
kept; the script prints the residuals). Median registration residuals are
~2 mm for the base, servo cap and head and ~1 mm for the arms, swivel and
neck (the STL and GLB tessellations differ, so ~1 mm is the floor; ICP samples
are random so the numbers move by a few tenths between runs). Only the base
shell (25 k faces) is decimated (`fast_simplification`, to 10 k); nothing
else is touched — removing the vendor parts' micro-triangles was tried and it
opens holes (0 → 390 boundary edges on the head), so they stay. Result:
3.3 MB, 0 boundary edges on every link except 35 on the head (the vendor's
`light-cover.stl` is not closed).
Servos, PCBs and screws are not modelled (they exist only in the GLB).
Masses/inertias in the URDF are nominal placeholders.
